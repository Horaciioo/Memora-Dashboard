# Type pairing reference

A pairing is a starting point for the brainstorm pass, not a lookup table to skip it. Pick from here
only after the brief has told you what tension you're going for (wet vs. dry, editorial vs. technical,
warm vs. authoritative) — never before. All faces below are on Google Fonts / Fontshare / the listed
foundry, so licensing is never the reason to fall back to a default.

The single most over-used pairing right now is **Inter for everything** or **Inter body + a generic
geometric-sans display**. It is not wrong, it is just invisible — treat it as the pairing you reach for
by default and actively look for a reason to reach for something else first.

## By tension

| Tension                                   | Display                                  | Body                           | Reads as                                                  |
| ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Wet serif vs. dry engineered sans         | **Fraunces** (high optical size, italic) | **Söhne** / **General Sans**   | hand-finished product against a precise spec sheet        |
| Broken / unstable vs. rigid mono          | **PP Editorial New** / **Redaction**     | **JetBrains Mono**             | art object vs. paperwork                                  |
| Humanist warmth vs. cold default          | **Newsreader** (regular, not light)      | **Atkinson Hyperlegible**      | legible, book-like, made for the reader, not the designer |
| Geometric quirk vs. clean grid            | **Space Grotesk**                        | **Inter** or **Source Sans 3** | technical product with one deliberate idiosyncrasy        |
| Friendly rounded vs. neutral              | **Outfit**                               | **Source Sans 3**              | approachable without going soft everywhere                |
| Brutalist efficiency                      | **Untitled Sans** (Klim)                 | **IBM Plex Sans**              | developer tool, no ornament                               |
| Modern tech, slightly warmer than default | **Aeonik**                               | **Inter**                      | breaks Inter's monotony without abandoning it             |
| Old-world craft vs. modern grid           | **Tiempos** (Klim)                       | **Calibre** (Klim)             | legacy institution presented cleanly                      |

## Foundries worth knowing beyond Google Fonts

Reach here when the brief calls for something a competitor is unlikely to also be running:

- **Klim** (Wellington) — Tiempos, Calibre, Untitled Sans. Understated, exactingly drawn; the default
  choice when "quietly expensive" is the brief.
- **Pangram Pangram** — modern, charismatic, several free-for-testing weights; good for startups that
  want personality without a full brand-type budget.
- **Colophon** — Aperçu, Mabry. Contemporary edge, slightly harder-edged than Klim's output.
- **Fatype** — typefaces with real historical grounding; used by Balenciaga, Condé Nast. Reach for this
  when the brief has an editorial or fashion register.

## Rules that don't change per pairing

- Two families, occasionally three if the third is a monospace reserved for data/metadata only.
- Set a real type scale (not four random sizes) and commit to specific weights — do not ship a family
  at every weight it offers.
- If the body face is chosen for an accessibility-sensitive audience (older users, low vision, a public
  service), accessibility research (e.g. Atkinson Hyperlegible's design rationale) is a legitimate and
  sufficient reason to override "distinctive" — say so explicitly in the plan, as in `examples/design-plans.md` brief 2.
